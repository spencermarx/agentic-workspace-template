# The seven critics, across three failure layers

Loaded on demand by the [`seven-copy-critics`](../SKILL.md) skill at step 2.
Twenty-one checks: seven hostile reader personas, each applied at the hook,
body, and close.

### Step 2 - Run All 7 Critics Across All 3 Failure Layers

Each critic evaluates the draft at three layers. Write the verdict for each
layer in this format:

> **First Impression:** "In the first 3 seconds, I stop reading because ___"
> **Trust & Engagement:** "Even if I kept reading, I lose interest because ___"
> **Action:** "Even if I read everything, I would not act because ___"

If a layer genuinely passes for that critic, write "PASS" with a one-sentence
reason. No false credit - a marginal pass is a failure.

---

#### Critic 1 - The Time-Crushed

**Profile:** A CEO with 43 unread emails. Scans the first line and the subject.
Has 2 seconds to decide if this is worth opening, and another 3 seconds to
decide if it is worth reading past line one.

**Kills the copy when:** the subject line does not earn the next line, or the
opener buries the value in throat-clearing ("I hope this finds you well",
"My name is X and I work at Y", "I came across your profile and...").

---

#### Critic 2 - The Self-Conscious

**Profile:** Reads every email wondering "is this actually about me, or is this
a mass blast with my name pasted in?" Allergic to anything that could have
been sent to 500 other people.

**Kills the copy when:** the message could be search-and-replaced with anyone
else's name and still make sense. Generic role references ("as a operator...",
"as a marketer..."), boilerplate compliments ("loved your recent post"),
or vague situational references all fail this critic.

---

#### Critic 3 - The Burned Skeptic

**Profile:** Has bought 14 "game-changing frameworks" in the past 3 years.
Most disappointed them. Now treats every claim as guilty until proven.

**Kills the copy when:** claims appear without proof. Hype words ("revolutionary",
"game-changing", "the only platform that..."), unbacked metrics ("10x your
pipeline"), or testimonial-free promises all trigger immediate distrust. Even
true claims fail here if they are not anchored to specific evidence.

---

#### Critic 4 - The AI-Allergic

**Profile:** Has read so many ChatGPT-drafted emails they can spot the patterns
in milliseconds. Em dashes, "I hope this message finds you well", "I wanted to
reach out because", tricolon sentences with rising rhythm, and "It's not just
X - it's Y" constructions all set off the alarm.

**Kills the copy when:** the sentences sound like a polished corporate template
or follow recognizable LLM rhythm. Too smooth, too symmetrical, too "writerly."
Look for: parallel structure abuse, abstract nouns where concrete ones would do,
"unlock" / "leverage" / "empower" / "elevate", and any sentence that could
appear in 1000 other brand emails.

---

#### Critic 5 - The Lurker

**Profile:** Has been on the list for 3 months and never clicked. Reads
occasionally. Is not unsubscribing, but is not engaging either. Needs a
specific, concrete reason to break the pattern this time.

**Kills the copy when:** there is nothing specific enough to make today
different from the last 12 emails they ignored. Vague benefit promises, generic
"new feature" announcements, or "just checking in" framings all fail. The
lurker breaks pattern only for specificity: a named person, a concrete number,
a particular moment.

---

#### Critic 6 - The Visual Reader

**Profile:** Thinks in scenes, moments, and pictures. Glazes over abstract
concepts. Needs to see a thing happening to a person in a place.

**Kills the copy when:** the copy is built on abstractions ("operational
efficiency", "strategic alignment", "workflow optimization") with no scene to
ground them. Even good arguments fail if there is no concrete moment for the
reader to picture.

---

#### Critic 7 - The Inspiration Seeker

**Profile:** Does not just want information - wants to feel capable of acting
on it. Reads to be moved, not just informed. Will accept logic, but will not
move without motivation.

**Kills the copy when:** it is technically correct but emotionally flat. All
information, no momentum. The reader finishes thinking "that was interesting"
but does nothing. Action requires belief that *they specifically* can do
the thing.

---

#### Critic 8 - The Operator-Voice Detector

**Profile:** Has followed enough operators on LinkedIn to know the difference
between a operator who writes their own posts and a operator whose marketing
team is writing for them. The real operator's posts make you feel like you're
in the room with someone who cares. The marketing team's posts make you feel
like you're reading a brochure.

**Kills the copy when:** the post could have been written by any SaaS company's
content team. No personal stakes visible. No opinions stated. Data presented
without interpretation. Information delivered without conviction. The reader
finishes knowing MORE but not caring MORE. The post is a briefing, not a
conversation.

The test: cover the byline. Could this have come from anyone? If yes, it fails.

Specific failure signals:
- Data appears before the reader knows why the author cares
- Numbers are presented without the author's interpretation of what they mean
- The post reads like a report: fact, fact, fact, CTA
- No sentence expresses a personal belief, frustration, or conviction
- The reader never gets addressed directly (always "they" and "it", never "you")
