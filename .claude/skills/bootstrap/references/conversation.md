# Phase 1: the conversation

Loaded on demand by the [`bootstrap`](../SKILL.md) skill. Read nothing while
this phase is running.

## Why this comes first

The interview this replaced opened with fifteen numbered questions before anyone
had looked at anything. Every one carried a recommendation, which was its best
property and is kept. But it asked the person to describe their business in the
template's vocabulary, and it asked them to settle structural questions that are
the agent's to settle.

Talking first fixes both. The person describes their world in their own words,
and the only things they are asked for are the things only they can know.

## The opening

One question. Not a round, not a list:

> Before I look at anything: what is this workspace for, and what do you
> actually spend your time on?

Then follow up on what they said, one thread at a time, the way a colleague
would. Let them talk past the question.

## The four things you are listening for

Do not ask these as a list. Reach each one as a follow-up.

**What the work is**, in their words. One sentence you could say back to them.

**What recurs.** The load-bearing one: *what do you have many of, where each one
accumulates its own context over time?* Clients, ventures, products, properties,
cases, campaigns, portfolio companies. That answer becomes a top-level plural
folder with one sub-folder and one leaf `CLAUDE.md` per instance, and everything
downstream falls out of it.

Reach it as a follow-up to what they described, never as a numbered question,
and **never by offering a taxonomy for them to recognise themselves in**. This
template ships no domain profiles for exactly that reason: a profile makes
someone find their business in a stranger's list, while the question is
answerable in four seconds if you just ask it.

Listen also for a *second* thing they have many of that does not nest under the
first. Usually the honest shape is a small stable catalog rather than a growing
set of context-accumulating folders, but that is a Phase 3 recommendation, not a
question to put to them now.

**Who else touches this**, and whether anyone needs a private area. You need a
count and a sense of whether the work splits by person.

**Where things already are.** Every time they name a place, write it down: a
sibling repo, a Drive folder, an old vault, a folder of proposals, a Notion
export, real work product, a signed agreement. This list is Phase 2's input and
the most valuable thing this phase produces.

## Capture their vocabulary verbatim

If they say "engagements", do not write "clients". If they say "matters", do not
write "cases". Their words seed `CONTEXT.md` and the first paragraph of the root
`CLAUDE.md`, and a workspace that speaks back to someone in their own language
is the difference between one they adopt and one they abandon.

Note the synonyms they reject too. "We call them engagements, not projects" is
two entries in the glossary, not one.

## What not to do here

Do not propose a tree. Do not name a folder. Do not ask about naming
conventions, lifecycle stages, the inside of one instance, cross-cutting
folders, which skills to keep, or where work gets tracked.

Every one of those is a decision you should make and defend in Phase 3 with
evidence behind it. Asking them here spends the person's attention on your half
of the job, and they will answer worse than you would, because they have not
seen the tree yet.

## Exit condition

Both of these, or the phase is not finished:

1. You can say back what they do in one sentence, in their words, and they would
   recognise it.
2. You hold at least one concrete place to go and look.

If the second is empty, ask straight out: *what should I read to understand this
properly?* A bootstrap with nothing to explore produces a workspace built
entirely on assumptions, and Phase 3 will have to label it that way.
