# the workspace Voice and Tone

The single source of truth for how the workspace sounds in writing.

Consolidate into this file as patterns stabilize. For now, the content-sprint sub-skills remain the operational guide for drafting; this file holds the durable voice principles only.

## Core voice attributes

| Attribute | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Direct** | Lead with the point. Cut filler. No throat-clearing, no preambles, no recaps. |
| **Operator-grounded** | Speak the language of someone running a service business (or building one). Specific, concrete, real-world. No marketing abstractions. |
| **Confident, not arrogant** | We have a strong opinion and we say it plainly. We don't hedge with "perhaps" or "maybe." We also don't flex or condescend. |
| **Curious** | We ask real questions. We're interested in how things actually work. |

## Hard rules

1. **No em dashes.** Restructure with periods, commas, or colons. (See `feedback_no_em_dashes.md` in agent memory.)
2. **No recapping.** Don't restate what someone already said back to them.
3. **Lead with dream outcome, not mechanism.** The reader wants to know what they get. The how is the explanation, not the hook.
4. **No AI tells.** No "I'd love to," "happy to chat," "circle back," "let me know your thoughts." No bullet lists where prose would work better.
5. **Specific over clever.** A specific business situation lands harder than a clever turn of phrase.
